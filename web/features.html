Demos

Demos were recorded from a real HX Stomp's screen, along with its audio and button presses, then overlaid on a simulated HX Stomp.

BPM Display

The Home screen shows the tempo at its top left, and a beat dot beside the preset number that lights with the TAP footswitch's ring, red on a tap.

  • The tempo is the one the HX Stomp is using, the global or the preset's as Tempo Select says, as a whole number. It shows X after it while the tempo comes from MIDI clock (the tempo screen's Extrnl), and -- with no tempo.
  • The dot keeps the beat in every footswitch mode, and with Tap LED Color on Off too: the ring then stays dark while the dot blinks.

Global Settings > BPM Display

Setting Values What it does
Font Size XL, L, M, S, Off The tempo's size. XL is digits only: no X with MIDI clock and nothing with no tempo. Off hides the tempo
Color White, Gray, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Cyan, Blue, Purple, Tempo The tempo's and the dot's colour. Tempo follows the TAP footswitch's colour (Tap LED Color; stock red in a build without it)
Dot Position Left, Right, Both, Off Where the beat dot sits, beside the preset number. Off hides the dot; with Font Size Off too the BPM display is off and the TAP ring blinks as stock

Breathing Line

Just for fun: the line across the middle of the Home screen, between the signal flow and the knobs, comes alive in the colours, motion and speed you pick. It starts on Rainbow, breathing once every 4 s.

  • Where it shows: on the Home screen only. Other screens stay stock, and the line stops animating while you're on them.
  • The selected part of the line is still thicker, as on the stock line, and Off gives the stock line back.

Compressor Gain Reduction Meter

A gain-reduction meter on the Home screen and in the compressor's Edit view, lit by the compressor you turn it on in, so you can see how hard it is working. HX Stomp only.

  • Which compressors: Deluxe, LA Studio, Kinky, Ampeg Opto, Rochester and Red Squeeze.
  • Edit view: while one of these compressors is selected, the meter shows in its Edit view too, in the same place and size, so you can set the compressor up while you play. Its lit lights cover what is under them (the preset name at Top, the knob names at Bottom); unlit ones leave it showing. With two metered compressors in a preset, both Edit views show the one whose Mtr Size was set last.
  • With NAM: the meter's DSP code takes the room of NAM A2 Lite's third block, so a build with it has two NAM blocks at most.

EXP Display

Where your expression pedals are, at a glance: a bar at each edge of the Home screen, from 0 at the bottom of the screen to 100 just under the line across the middle, and above that line, beside the signal flow, EXP1 or EXP2 over the value from 0 to 100. Both are in the pedal's colour: EXP 1 red, EXP 2 green, as TogglEXP lights them. Both sides start Off: pick a pedal for each in Global Settings > Displays.

  • The value is the one your blocks get from EXP 1 or EXP 2, in the HX Stomp's 1% steps. MIDI CC 1 and 2, which move EXP 1 and EXP 2, move it too. A pedal reads 0 (100 with its Polarity Inverted) until you first sweep it after plugging it in, as the HX Stomp sends that to your blocks. With EXP 1 or EXP 2 Position saved per preset or per snapshot (Global Settings > EXP Pedals), changing preset, or snapshot when it is per snapshot, gives your blocks the saved position: the bar then shows only its outline, in the pedal's colour, until you move the pedal, while the value shows where the pedal is.
  • With TogglEXP and one pedal, the side the pedal no longer moves turns grey and keeps its last value.
  • With no pedal (or its jack set to a footswitch) and nothing from MIDI in the last 3 seconds, a side shows an empty bar and --.
  • With the gain reduction meter on the Home screen, the bars and values move aside for it: at Bottom the bars start just above it, at Left or Right that side moves in, and at Middle the bars end just below it.
  • Where it shows: on the Home screen only. A long footswitch name can run under the inner edge of a bar.

DSP Usage Display

In the Edit view, each block in the signal-flow row shows its share of the DSP in per cent, in black on the block. The top right corner shows the blocks' total out of 75, e.g. 47/75.

In the model browser, each model shows the share it would take, in white between its icon and its name, and the top right corner shows the total as in the Edit view.

75 is what blocks may use: the firmware's preset limit of 90%, less the 15% it always keeps. The numbers are the firmware's own fixed cost of each model, the same ones it uses to decide whether a block fits, not a measured load.

Extra tuner settings

The tuner's own settings get a page of their own, third: page right past Trails, Offsets and String 6. The string pages (Strings 5–3, then 2 and 1) follow it: few players use the per-string offsets, so the page comes before them. Its knobs set them as the stock pages' knobs do: turn to change, press to go back to the default.

Foot Switch Long Press

In Stomp footswitch mode, each of FS1–FS5 can have a Hold action (Global Settings > Footswitches, pages 2 and 3: FS1 Hold Function … FS5 Hold Function). Holding the switch for 1 s runs it; a tap still toggles the switch's own blocks, on release instead of on press, since the press can't tell yet whether it will become a hold. A switch with a looper on it (1 Switch Looper or Shuffling Looper) ignores its Hold action and works as stock, so the looper keeps its own press, undo and clear.

  • Tuner opens the tuner, as holding TAP does.
  • FS4, FS5 press that footswitch, as an external switch would.
  • FS6 … FS10 switch a virtual footswitch: see Virtual Footswitches (FS6-10).
  • A switch with Hold Off, a momentary switch, and the Preset and Snapshot footswitch modes work as stock.

FX Loop Noise Gate

A noise gate built into the FX Loop and Return blocks, as the Input block has one, so a noisy pedal in the loop doesn't take a Noise Gate block of its own (#69). It gates what comes back from the loop, before the block's Return level and Mix; the dry signal and the Send are not gated.

  • DSP: each FX Loop and Return block counts 0.2% more toward the DSP limit (0.4% stereo), whether its gate is on or off. A preset already at the limit with one of these blocks may not load.
  • Stereo blocks gate each side on its own, not linked.
  • Presets saved with the gate keep its knobs. Opening such a preset on stock firmware, or in HX Edit, is not tested yet.

Settings

The block's Gate, Threshold and Decay knobs

Knob Values What it does
Gate Off, On Turns the gate on. Off, the block sounds as on stock firmware.
Threshold −96 … −48 … 0 dB Below this level the returned signal is muted.
Decay 0.01 … 0.1 … 1 s How fast the gate closes.

Metronome

A click track in time with the tempo, as a block: add it from the model browser, Volume/Pan > Metronome > Metronome, anywhere in the signal flow. It is a mono effect block, in a folder of its own beside Volume/Pan's Mono and Stereo. In the signal flow it is teal, lit when on and dim when off, and its icon is a metronome.

  • The beat starts when the block does, and a new tempo or Time counts from the last beat. Tapping the tempo doesn't yet move the beat onto your taps, and BPM Display's dot blinks with the TAP ring, not with the click.
  • Presets store the block as model 181, as the earlier builds that listed it under Modulation did, so presets made with them keep it.
  • In a stereo rig, the Metronome sums the signal to mono while it is on, as any mono block does. Put it before the stereo delays and reverbs, or alone on path B with Thru Off, where only the click joins at the Mixer.
  • On path B, a bypassed block passes the guitar again, so the Mixer's B Level goes down with the Metronome. Select the Mixer (where the paths merge) and set A Level to +3.0 dB, to make up for the 3 dB its centre pan takes off path A. Hold Knob 3 (B Level), set Controller to the Metronome's footswitch, then on the next page Min Value −60.0 dB and Max Value 0.0 dB. If the ring is lit while the click is silent, select the Metronome, press the Upper Knob once, and save.

Settings

The block's Time, Level, Accent and Thru knobs

Knob Values What it does
Time 1/4 and the other note values, synced to the tempo as a delay's Time The beat: 100 ms to 8 s.
Level −60 … −14 … +6 dB The click's volume.
Accent Off, 2 … 12 Every nth beat is a downbeat, a higher click (2 kHz instead of 1 kHz).

More Snapshots (4‑6)

With Footswitch Mode on Snapshot, the three footswitches select snapshots 1–3 as usual. Press the lit one again and the same footswitches select snapshots 4–6; press the lit one again to come back.

  • Which bank you're in: in 4–6, the camera icon at the top right and the footswitch labels show 4, 5 or 6.
  • New snapshots: the first time you flip to a preset's 4–6, each one starts as a copy of the snapshot you flipped from. Edit them as any snapshot: snapshot-controlled parameters, block bypass, tempo and names are kept per snapshot.
  • Saving: a save keeps all six with the preset. Saving while in 4–6, you hear the matching snapshot of 1–3 for a moment, as the save writes 1–3 first.
  • Stock firmware and HX Edit: a preset saved with 4–6 still loads on a stock firmware, as snapshots 1–3, and the stock firmware drops 4–6 if it saves the preset again. HX Edit opens, exports and imports these presets, but its exports (.hlx) and backups (.hxb) hold snapshots 1–3 only. Snapshots 4–6 live on the HX Stomp.
  • Next and previous: once a preset has snapshots 4–6 (saved with it, or made by a flip since it loaded), the SnpshtUp and SnpshtDn footswitch functions and MIDI CC 69's next and previous (values 8 and 9) step through all six: next on 3 goes to 4 and next on 6 to 1; previous on 1 goes to 6 and previous on 4 to 3. A preset with only 1–3 wraps within them, as stock.
  • MIDI CC 69: values 0–2 select snapshots 1–3 and values 3–5 select snapshots 4–6, whichever bank you're in. Selecting 4–6 on a preset that has none yet makes them as a flip does. Sending the lit snapshot's own value does what Reselect Snapshot says, as stock.
  • What else changes: pressing the lit snapshot footswitch no longer does what Reselect Snapshot says. HX Edit's snapshot buttons work within the bank you're in.

NAM A2 Lite

From upstream hx-stomp-nam-lite, unchanged in this fork but for how the Model knob reads: Neural Amp Modeler A2 Lite captures running on the HX Stomp's own DSP. The setup page converts your .nam files into User IRs the block reads.

  • Where: Amp > NAM, a block with ten knobs and a tone stack. It sits on Xenomorph Fuzz Mono's preset ID 555 (that stock model is removed), so HX Edit keeps NAM blocks, and presets made with either build open with the other.
  • Older presets: presets saved with an earlier NAM build (preset IDs 147 and 148) don't open NAM A2 Lite in this one: re-add the block in those presets.
  • How many blocks: up to 3 play at once, each its own capture; a further one passes its input through. Fewer leave room for more of the other DSP features: 3 blocks leave room for the Metronome and the FX Loop gate, or for USB & Send Routing or Input Sidechain alone; 2 for all five. With build.py, HX_NAM_SLOTS sets it; unset, it's the most that leave room for the DSP features you turned on.
  • Captures: none are included. You bring your own .nam files, and their licences are up to their authors.

Upstream's CHANGELOG.md has the NAM changes.

Noise Gate Input Sidechain

The Noise Gate block can take its key from the input instead of from its own signal (#131). With Input Sidechain on, the gate opens and closes on what the Input block's gate hears (your guitar before any block, left and right summed) while it still gates the signal reaching it. A gate after a high-gain drive then follows the clean guitar: it closes when you stop playing, and the drive's own hiss no longer holds it open.

  • Experimental: tested with recorded signals, not yet with a played instrument, so it may need tuning. It works only with a cable in the HX Stomp's guitar input.
  • DSP: each Noise Gate block counts 0.1% more toward the DSP limit (0.2% stereo), whether Input Sidechain is on or off. A preset already at the limit with a Noise Gate may not load.
  • Threshold is then compared with the input's level, so set it as you would the Input block's gate. The Input block's own Input Gate can be on or off.
  • Presets saved with Input Sidechain keep the knob. HX Edit doesn't show it, as it has its own list of each block's knobs; opening such a preset on stock firmware is not tested yet.

Preset Mode LED Color

In Preset footswitch mode the HX Stomp lights the current preset's ring red, with a red switch icon on the Home screen, and leaves the other two presets' rings off. HXtra gives each preset a colour of its own, from the same palette as Tap LED Color.

  • This Preset (Global Settings > Displays, page 2) sets the current preset's colour. Save the preset to keep it: the colour is stored in the preset itself, so it goes with the preset when you move, export or restore it.
  • Selected, a preset's ring and icon are lit in its colour. Not selected, a preset with a colour shows it dimmed (the shade the HX Stomp uses for a bypassed block of that colour), so you can see which footswitch holds which preset.
  • A preset without a colour is lit in Preset LED's colour (red by default, as stock) while it is selected, and off while it is not.
  • A change shows when you go back to the Home screen.
  • Other modes keep the stock colours: Stomp mode's rings are the blocks' colours, Snapshot mode's the snapshots'.

Real Block Names

Line 6 names its models after the gear they're based on with a twist (Deez One Mod, US Double Nrm). With Real block names, the model browser and the Edit view title each block by that gear instead: Boss DS-1 Mod, Twin Reverb Nrm. The names come from helixhelp.com/models' "based on", at most 16 characters.

  • What keeps its name: Line 6 originals, utility blocks and titles that are already real.
  • Presets keep the same model IDs, so a preset is the same with or without real names, and opens on stock firmware as usual.

Tap Command

On a stock HX Stomp, tap tempo is a global footswitch function: FS3's TAP/TUNER, or FS4/FS5 set to TAP, in every preset. HXtra adds a Tap command to the Command Center, which is saved with each preset, so a footswitch taps the tempo only in the presets where you put it.

  • Setting it up: the footswitch has to be a Stomp switch, so use Stomp footswitch mode, where FS1 and FS2 always are; for FS3, FS4 or FS5 set its function to Stomp in Global Settings > Footswitches. Then, in the preset, open Menu > Command Center, select the footswitch's slot and turn Command to Tap (after Hotkey). Save the preset to keep it.
  • Pressing it taps the tempo exactly as the TAP footswitch does. Holding it doesn't tap.
  • Its ring (FS1–FS3) blinks the tempo as the TAP ring does, in Tap LED Color's colour when that feature is in your build. Tap LED Color's Off stops it blinking (in a build without Tap LED Color, the stock Tap Tempo LED does). FS4 and FS5 have no ring.
  • Changing snapshots never taps.
  • The Home screen label reads Tap, as a footswitch with another command reads that command's name; Customize in the Command Center renames it.
  • On stock firmware a preset with a Tap command opens as usual, and the footswitch doesn't tap.

TAP LED Color

The TAP footswitch's ring blinks red with the tempo, and its icon and label on the Home screen are red. Tap LED Color (Global Settings > Displays, in Tap Tempo LED's place) picks another colour for all three, from the colours the HX Stomp gives its other footswitches.

  • The colours are the stock footswitch palette: each ring colour is the one the HX Stomp lights for a block of that colour, and each icon the stock switch icon of that colour.
  • Rainbow moves the ring a small step round the colour wheel at each beat: 42 shades blended from the palette's Red, DkOrange, Yellow, Green, Blue and Violet, back to Red, about 21 s round at 120 BPM.
  • Random lights the ring in a random colour of the palette at each beat, never the same twice running.
  • With Rainbow and Random, the icon and label stay White, and a tap or a preset change re-lights the ring in its current colour without moving on.
  • External MIDI clock: the ring keeps the stock colour it blinks with then. Off stops the blink: Tap LED Color takes the stock Tap Tempo LED's place in Displays and sets it, and a unit that had Tap Tempo LED Off starts on Off.
  • BPM Display's Color Tempo gives the tempo and the beat dot the TAP footswitch's colour, the ring's current one with Rainbow and Random.

USB & Send Routing

The HX Stomp always plays what you play straight to its outputs and phones, and sends the same sound to the computer on USB 1/2: hardware monitoring. To monitor through a DAW instead (software monitoring: hearing your tone with the DAW's plugins on it), set Path A Out (Global Settings > Ins/Outs, page 3) to Send/USB. Path A's Output block then goes to the computer on USB 3/4 instead of to Main L/R, and Main and the phones play only what the computer sends back on USB 1/2. Record and monitor USB 3/4 in the DAW.

  • Every preset follows the setting: path A goes to Send/USB on each preset you load, and a turn of the setting switches at once. Path B's own output is not changed.
  • The Send jacks carry the same sound as USB 3/4: on the HX Stomp they are one output. Leave them unplugged, or expect the sound there too.
  • Send and FX Loop blocks add their send to that output, so the computer gets it with path A's sound. Don't use Send/USB with an FX Loop block (or the 4-cable method): path A's output would go out the Send jacks, through the loop and back in, and feed back.
  • USB Only sends path A to the computer alone. The Send jacks stay silent, so nothing goes out of them at all: not path A, and not the send of any Send or FX Loop block either. That makes it safe with an FX Loop block in the preset (nothing can feed back), but the loop gets no signal. Choose it when you only monitor through the DAW.
  • Main+Snd keeps path A on Main L/R and sends what Main plays to the Send jacks and USB 3/4 as well, at 0 dB, without a split or a block of its own: for a second amp, a front-of-house feed or recording the whole chain. Anything a Send or FX Loop block sends is added to it. With the Volume knob on the phones only (Global Settings > Ins/Outs), the knob doesn't change it. Global EQ applies to it on the Send jacks, as it does to Main, but not on USB 3/4. Don't use Main+Snd with an FX Loop block either: Main carries the loop's return, which would go back out the Send jacks and feed back.
  • Saving a preset with Send/USB or USB Only on stores Send for path A. A stock HX Stomp refuses that value when it loads the preset and keeps path A on Main L/R.
  • The stock firmware can reach the same output only from path B: put a block on path B, move the merge to the end (its output is then Send L/R) and turn path A's Output Level down.

USB Only sends it to the computer alone, the Send jacks silent. Main+Snd keeps it on Main L/R and copies Main to the Send jacks and USB 3/4 too.

Virtual Footswitches (FS6‑10)

FS6–FS10 are five more footswitches with no switch of their own. Hold a real one to press one (Foot Switch Long Press), or send MIDI CC 20–24. Each has a function (Global Settings > Footswitches, pages 4 and 5: FS6 Function … FS10 Function).

  • FS6 … FS10 are virtual footswitches. Each toggles the blocks you assign to it: in a block's Bypass Assign, set MIDI In to FS6 … FS10 (the first five steps up from Off; the knob's label reads FootswIn while it's on one). The assignment is saved in the preset, and parameters assigned to the same footswitch through Controller Assign follow it too. They are MIDI CC 20–24 underneath, which is how HX Edit and other MIDI gear see them.
  • FS6 Function … FS10 Function give a virtual footswitch the same choices FS3 … FS5 Function give a real one, with Tuner (opens the tuner) in place of TAP/Tunr, since a hold has no use for a tap. Stomp, the default, toggles its blocks as above. The others do what that function does on a press and release of a real footswitch: Bank Up/Dn opens the bank in Preset mode, PresetUp/Dn and SnpshtUp/Dn step the preset or snapshot, FS Mode> / <FS Mode step the footswitch mode, AllBypas bypasses everything, and TogglEXP switches EXP 1/2 and toggles the virtual footswitch's blocks. GlobalEQ, which no real footswitch offers, opens Global EQ for room or stage correction; it does nothing while the tuner is open. While Global EQ is open, however you got there, a press of any footswitch goes back to Home, as one leaves the tuner, and toggles nothing. Holds act only in Stomp mode, so after a function that leaves it (FS Mode, Bank), come back with a real footswitch or the menu. A MIDI CC 20–24 from outside still toggles the blocks, whatever the function.
